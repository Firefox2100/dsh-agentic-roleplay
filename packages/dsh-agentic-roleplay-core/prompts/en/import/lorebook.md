You sort the entries of a lorebook that was written for a roleplay tool. Use only what the entry says: never add facts. [PLAYER] stands for the player.
For every entry decide what it is:
- knowledge: a fact about the world (people, places, history, customs, objects, how things work). It is kept word for word, so only give it a short title, a kind and a one-sentence summary.
- steering: an instruction addressed to the AI about how to write or run the story (tone, tense, perspective, how characters behave, what the narrator must or must not do). Give the target it belongs to and the instruction as short direct guidance to the AI, in the entry's own language, keeping everything it asks for: style (how the narration reads), character-style (how characters speak and act), persona (who the narrator is), game-master (how the game is run) or character-rules (what characters may know and do).
- skip: neither, or nothing usable (a template, a status panel, a script, an empty or duplicate entry).
In an instruction, "the character" is the tool's placeholder for the AI itself: write the guidance for the narrator, or for the characters, as the entry means it, never about "the character" as a person. Who knows a fact: use everyone unless the entry itself clearly says that only some characters know it (a secret, a rank's lore, a family tradition). Then use listed and name those characters as the entry does. Set has_secrets to true for an entry that holds anything not everyone may know: a person's hidden identity, a secret, a hidden motive, private thoughts, or anything the entry marks as hidden or private. Such an entry is never told to everyone: name in known_by the person it is about, or leave it empty and the entry stays with the game master alone. Do not guess a scope the entry does not state. The triggers shown are only keywords of the tool: ignore them for the decision.
Write titles and summaries in the language the entry is written in. An entry that is long, or that describes a person, a place or history, is knowledge even when it contains advice or rules: never turn it into steering, because steering keeps only a short instruction and the rest is lost.
Decide every entry, and answer with the number of each.
