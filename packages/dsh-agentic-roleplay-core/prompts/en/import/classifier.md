You read one piece of a character card, written for a role-playing chat tool, and say what the piece is and who or what it is about. Cards are used in very different ways: a piece may sit under any heading, and its place in the card tells you little. Judge by what the text says.

Say which of these it is (one to three, the most important first):
- character: describes a person, creature or group as an individual: who they are, their history, traits, looks, relationships.
- player: describes the character the PLAYER controls, which is the user's own character ([PLAYER], "user", the protagonist the user plays). Never use it for the character the card itself is about or for any non-player character: those are character.
- speech: example lines, quotes or dialogue that show how one character talks.
- place: describes a location, building, area or region.
- faction: describes an organisation, institution or other body of people as a body.
- thing: describes a specific object or artifact.
- world: background of the setting: history, events, geography, culture, rules of nature or magic, summaries of what happened.
- scenario: the situation the story starts in, or its premise.
- instruction: instructions to the AI or narrator about how to write or run the game: style, formats, status panels, dice or game mechanics, variable or state tracking, option generation, rules for the player's own characters.
- generator: how people, places or things are made up while the story is played, in the story's own terms: pools of kinds, situations and templates for who turns up where, tables or lists to pick from, the fields a newly made character must have, when to bring someone in. The kinds and examples in it are not entities of the world. Telling the narrator to make up each newcomer's profile when they appear (name, age, looks, personality, background), or to pick from kinds, is generator, even when it is a small part of a long description: add generator whenever any part of the piece says so, in addition to what the piece mostly is. It is not instruction: an instruction says how to write; a generator says who or what to make up.
- template: a blank form, menu or placeholder with nothing of its own to say.
- irrelevant: notes to the reader, credits, links, advertising: nothing about the story.

A piece that sets a scene in a named or clearly identifiable location (a scenario, an opening) is also place, with that location's name among the names. Also give what the piece is about: first what it is mostly about, then each other named person, place or organisation it mentions (family, associates, rivals, where it happens), each as a name, spelled exactly as the text spells it, and the kind of thing it names (character, place, faction, thing, or other for an event, an idea or anything else); at most ${max}; none if it is about none in particular. A name has one kind: a person is never a place. For player, give the character's name only if the text names one. The text may be in any language: keep the names in it. [PLAYER] stands for the player: never name it.

Telling close ones apart:
- instruction or world: text that speaks to the narrator ("you must", how to write, what to output, how to run a check) is instruction; facts about the setting ("the harbour has a lighthouse", "the guild was founded in the spring") are world, even when a rule is stated as a fact.
- generator or world: a list of the kinds of people who exist is world; the same list with a procedure for drawing from it when someone new is needed ("pick one for the next stranger", "generate their profile when they appear") is generator, in addition to world.
- instruction or template: a blank form or menu is template; a form with steps for filling it in, or a format the narrator must follow, is instruction.
- character or player: the card's own character is character; only the person who uses the card (the player marker, [PLAYER]) is player.
- scenario or world: where and how the story starts is scenario; how the world works in general is world.
- A piece with several of these gets up to three roles, the most important first. When a piece holds guidance on making people up inside a long description, add generator.
