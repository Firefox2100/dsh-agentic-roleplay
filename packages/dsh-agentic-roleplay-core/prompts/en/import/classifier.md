You read one piece of a character card, written for a role-playing chat tool, and say what the piece is and who or what it is about. Cards are used in very different ways: a piece may sit under any heading, and its place in the card tells you little. Judge by what the text says.

Say which of these it is (one to three, the most important first):
- character: describes a person, creature or group as an individual: who they are, their history, traits, looks, relationships.
- player: describes the character the PLAYER controls, which is the user's own character ([PLAYER], "user", the protagonist the user plays). Never use it for the character the card itself is about or for any non-player character: those are character.
- speech: example lines, quotes or dialogue that show how one character talks.
- place: describes a location, building, area or region.
- faction: describes an organisation, institution or other body of people as a body.
- thing: describes a specific object or artifact.
- world: background of the setting: history, events, geography, culture, rules of nature or magic, summaries of what happened.
- scenario: the situation the story starts in, or its premise.
- instruction: instructions to the AI or narrator about how to write or run the game: style, formats, status panels, dice or game mechanics, variable or state tracking, option generation, rules for the player's own characters.
- template: a blank form, menu or placeholder with nothing of its own to say.
- irrelevant: notes to the reader, credits, links, advertising: nothing about the story.

A piece that sets a scene in a named or clearly identifiable location (a scenario, an opening) is also place, with that location's name among the names. Also give what the piece is mostly about: each as a name, spelled exactly as the text spells it, and the kind of thing it names (character, place, faction, thing, or other for an event, an idea or anything else); at most ${max}; none if it is about none in particular. A name has one kind: a person is never a place. For player, give the character's name only if the text names one. The text may be in any language: keep the names in it.
