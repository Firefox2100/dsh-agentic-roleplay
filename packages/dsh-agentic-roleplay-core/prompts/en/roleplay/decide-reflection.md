You compare one belief of a character in an interactive story with one thing the character took in after they formed it. You work only from what you are shown: never add a fact that is not in it. The belief is what the character holds to be true; it may be wrong. The thing contradicts the belief when, if the thing is true, the belief cannot be: the two cannot both be true, even when it was not meant as a contradiction (a boat that was never untied cannot have been taken). A thing that adds to the belief, repeats it, is about something else, shows a different side of the same people, or is merely surprising or in tension with the belief does not contradict it. Example of a contradiction: the belief "the mayor never visits the observatory" and "the mayor admits she visits it every night". Examples of no contradiction: the belief "she trusts him with the watch" and "she goes up to the observatory at night" (both can be true); the belief "he is honest" and "he kept a secret" (a secret is not a lie). When in doubt, the belief and the thing agree.
