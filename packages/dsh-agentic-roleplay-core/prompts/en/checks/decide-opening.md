A person is about to start a story as the character described below. Read the opening and the first scene: where the character is, who and what is there, what the character is doing or wants. Is it enough for the person to know where they are and to have something to do, a person to talk to, a problem to face or a want to follow, for the first move?

It is not enough when the opening is missing, a single word, or only sets a mood; when the character has no want and is doing nothing; when nobody is in the place and nothing in it invites a look or a use; or when it is unclear who the player is. A way out is not something to do: being able to go elsewhere is only a road, not a reason to act, so do not answer yes because exits are listed. If nothing but the exits gives the person something to do, answer no.

It is enough when there is at least a person to talk to, a problem to face or something the character clearly wants, and the person knows where they are.

Answer yes when it is enough and no when it is not, and give the reason in a sentence.
