The roleplay session is a normal DSH chat using the `roleplay` agent preset. The game master responds with structured narration rendered as prose and named character utterances.

## Sending actions

Write what your character says, attempts or pays attention to. You may combine action and dialogue. The GM determines consequences, chooses which autonomous characters should act, and may run more than one character round before narrating.

You do not need to restate character sheets, scene facts or earlier events. The engine assembles current state and relevant memories for the agents. Specific actions still produce better play than abstract instructions: “ask Mara where she found the key” gives the scene more to resolve than “advance the plot.”

Out-of-character messages are allowed when they begin with a command, and are a turn of their own:

- Type a slash in the message box and the commands of the chat are listed with a short description: `/ooc`, `/ask`, `/roll`, `/wait` and `/dream` in every story, and the commands the world defines in that world's chat. A command that cannot be played (an empty `/ooc`, `/wait` with no time) answers at once and costs no turn.
- `/ooc <command>` speaks to the game master as the author of the story ("Claire goes backstage", "there is a key under the table"). The game master first rules whether it breaks a rule of the world or your own rules; if not, it makes it happen with the ordinary checks and records, and you read a note beside the narration.
- `/ask <question>` asks the game master something about the story ("does Alice know where Bob went?"). The answer comes from what you have been shown and what your character has seen, heard, been told or could know; other characters' secrets and what the story has not revealed stay unsaid. Nobody acts, nothing changes and no story time passes.

Both can be taken back, regenerated and branched like any other turn.

Letting time pass is a command of its own, and an ordinary turn, not an out-of-character one:

- `/dream [name]` takes you into the dream of a character who is asleep now (their day says they sleep, and they are where they sleep, and nothing has woken them). Name them when more than one is asleep. The sleeper is asked what the dream holds by the program itself, and the game master narrates it as a dream: blurred, shifting, sideways. A dream changes nothing in the waking world, and the sleeper stays asleep. It says so when nobody is asleep, and in a chat whose characters keep no days nobody is.
- `/wait <time> [why]` lets that much story time pass (`/wait 2 hours`, `/wait 30 minutes until the ferry`, `/wait 3 days`; units in English or Chinese). The program moves the story's clock on by that much itself, with the characters keeping to their days meanwhile, and the game master narrates what the time held, asking only the characters the time reaches; if something comes up that you must decide, it lets it happen at the end and stops there. It plays at most as many rounds as the chat's round limit allows, and cannot skip more than *Longest skip (days)* in the chat's time settings, nor skip time in a chat that follows the wall clock; it tells you when it cannot.

## Additional tabs

- **World** shows and edits the world or current session state.
- **Chat settings** changes per-session orchestration, memory, context, media and prompt settings.
- **Gallery** shows generated and uploaded images with their metadata. It is a page of the right sidebar, so it can stay open beside the story.

Suggested actions are optional prompts, not forced choices. The user can always enter a different action. While rounds are still waiting to be written into the story's memory, a line above the suggestions says how many; it goes away once the record keeper has written them.

## Prompts page

**Role Play → Prompts** is where you read and edit the instructions the models are given. A **≠** marks a prompt that differs from the one this version ships, which means you edited it or the shipped text changed after the file was copied. Open one to see your file beside the shipped text (lines yours lacks are highlighted), save your edits, or replace your file with the shipped text after a confirmation; the replaced file is kept in `.trash/prompts/` in your roleplay root. See [the prompt system](../specifications/prompt-system#the-prompts-page).

## Reference sidebar

Open the right sidebar's new-tab menu and choose **Reference**. It contains:

- **Profile**: your character's appearance, personality, background, speech, current state, goals, groups, memories and beliefs.
- **People**: characters you have met or who are with you. Select a name to see what your character can observe about them.
- **Relations**: the people you have met drawn around your character, each line naming what they are to you.
- **Status**: every tracked value you can read, by subject: the world's, your own character's (private ones too), the place's, those of the people you have met or are with, and of the things you carry or see lying about, each at its current value even when that is the starting one, and without the cap that the game master's text keeps. Hidden values and other people's private ones never show. The page is the place for a world that tracks many values or different ones for different characters; the interface pack's strip is for a few small ones.
- **Quests**: what your character has promised, arranged or been told of that is not finished: appointments and promises they are part of (soonest first, with when, where and who with), and the story's threads they know of, including settled ones. A thread the story keeps to itself never shows here.
- **Places** and **Map**: places and routes your character knows. Closed routes are dashed on the map.
- **Things**: what you carry, wear or can see nearby.
- **Journal**: private notes saved with the story. The story never reads them. Rewinding leaves them alone, while a new branch starts with a copy.

The sidebar follows your character's knowledge. It does not reveal hidden objects, private thoughts or unknown places. Your profile also leaves out whether a memory is accurate, since the character cannot know that. Reference pages refresh while open; the journal changes only when you edit it.

## What happens after sending

The activity indicator may cover more than one model request. The GM decides who matters, selected character agents answer from their own perspectives, the GM resolves outcomes, and the record keeper may persist events and perceptions. Optional suggestion, speech, image and indexing work can follow. The latest helper activity and failures are visible in **Chat settings**.

If a response fails before delivery, the turn is not silently presented as complete. Retry after correcting the provider or configuration problem. Story state is committed through explicit tools rather than inferred from partially displayed prose.

## Playing out of character

Use a clearly marked instruction such as `OOC:` when asking for a presentation change, clarification or pause. OOC instructions are guidance to the GM, not a hidden administrator command. Use the World tab when you intentionally want to inspect or edit session state.

Characters know only their own relationships, perceptions, beliefs and explicit entity knowledge. They should not act on facts merely because those facts exist in the world database.
