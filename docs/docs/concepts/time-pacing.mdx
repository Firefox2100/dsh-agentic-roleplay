## Story clock

In `simulated` mode, time advances by the duration the GM records for rounds and explicit skips. In `real` mode, story time follows the wall clock in the configured IANA time zone. Once a simulated story has its own dates, it cannot be changed back to real time.

Memory age, deadlines, routines and off-stage work use story time, not turn count or elapsed real time. A hundred rapid turns can occupy one story minute; a single skip may move several days.

## Routines and off-stage action

Routine windows describe where a character normally is and what it does. They provide a default, not a rail: an ongoing commitment, interruption or story event can override the routine. When the way to the place a window names has a travel time on every exit, a character away from you is on the way ("on the way to the Market") until that long after the window began, and only then there; with no timed way it arrives at once.

Authors can limit a routine by weekday, month, recurring or one-time date, date span, day of the month, season or a holiday declared by the world. Exclusions make cases such as “weekdays except Founder's Day” deterministic. The first window of the list that fits a day applies, so a one-day change is a window dated with `on` (for example `"on": ["1912-10-05"]`) placed before the usual ones. In play, the record keeper does the same with `routine_exceptions` when the story establishes that one character's day differs on one date (ill in bed, away on an errand): it puts a window for that date in front of the routine and leaves every other day alone; setting the same date again replaces it. Seasons follow the hemisphere in the world's `calendar.json`; see [Calendar and day rules](../world-format/calendar).

After a turn, a bounded number of off-stage characters may act when commitments or deadlines become due. Their consequences enter the same record system as on-stage action.

The people of one off-stage commitment decide one after another, each told who has already meant to come, so that a meeting is between them rather than two guesses (setting `offstage.joint`). Organisations act on their own too: after a message, up to `factions.perTurn` organisations that have a goal in hand and whose last decision is `factions.everyDays` of story time old decide one concrete thing they do. It changes their state and goals and is recorded as a deed that no character knows until someone is told.

Nothing runs while you are away from the chat, and each message you send lets only a few characters act, once each, so leaving a session open never spends tokens. Those characters also tell, in the same request, what came of it in one line; with who moved by routine, it appears as a *Meanwhile* card beside the story until your next message. The card is not in the conversation log, so it costs nothing in later turns.

## Pacing

Plots contain optional beats and pressure, while the pacing system decides whether the current moment needs a nudge. It considers recent movement, cooldown, introduced beats and explicit rejection. The objective is to restore direction, not finish plots quickly.

Triggers are different: they implement deterministic reactions when conditions become true. A trigger should be used when authorial intent requires the reaction every time; a plot beat is material the GM may adapt or defer.
