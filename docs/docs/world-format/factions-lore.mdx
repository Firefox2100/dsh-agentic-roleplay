## Factions

Factions live at `factions/<folder>/faction.json` and may represent institutions, families, movements or informal alliances.

```json
{
  "id": "observatory-trust",
  "name": "Blackwater Observatory Trust",
  "summary": "The body that controls access to the observatory.",
  "description": "Publicly charitable and privately divided.",
  "values": ["scientific prestige", "secrecy"],
  "structure": "A board chaired by the town magistrate.",
  "start": {
    "public_state": "Preparing its centenary exhibition.",
    "inner_state": "Trying to suppress records of the 1891 incident.",
    "goals": [{"id": "contain-records", "name": "Contain the records", "plan": "Recover missing files."}]
  }
}
```

Characters may belong to zero or more factions. Membership has its own role, rank and status. Faction-to-faction relationships are directional, like character relationships. Goals belong to the faction rather than being duplicated across members.

## Lore

Lore entries hold canonical setting facts that are not better represented as an entity or event. Each entry has a stable key, title, kind, summary, body, subjects, related entries and optional sources.

Characters do not automatically know canonical lore, unless an entry says so. An entry's `scope` is `listed` (the default in a world's own files): a `knowledge.lore` edge, or a name in the entry's `known_by` (character ids, names or aliases of the world), grants access to it. An entry whose `scope` is `everyone` is common knowledge that every character can recall; `known_by` is then not allowed. The GM may search all lore; character recall is restricted to the entries a character knows. Entries brought in from a [lorebook](../authoring/lorebooks) are `everyone` unless they say otherwise.

Use separate entries for facts that may be learned independently. Link their subjects to characters, locations, items or factions so retrieval can find relevant material.
